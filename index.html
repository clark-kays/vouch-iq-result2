01 — Our Recommendation

Our Recommendation

CoinX's program is a Web3 Protection Policy bound through Arthur J Gallagher with United Specialty Insurance Company. It runs 08/19/2025 to 08/19/2026 at a $12,017 total premium. The form does some real work: an 8-hour Cyber business interruption waiting period, $1M Privacy and Network Security Liability, and a standalone Tech E&O tower at $1M with a $25K retention. Compared to where the 2026 market is for a venture-backed web3 platform, several pieces of the program don't hold up.

Three structural issues define this analysis. First, every primary tower is bound at $1M, and the loss-prone coverages (Cyber Crime, Public Relations) are sublimited well below that. Second, the form carries a stack of web3-targeted exclusions (Tracking Tech, Biometric, AML, ICO, Transferor Errors) that, taken together, neutralize coverage for several of the highest-frequency claim scenarios in crypto today. Third, three lines Vouch flags as MUST HAVE aren't on the program at all: Media Liability, Commercial Crime, and D&O.

Priority recommendations
1
Increase Tech E&O from $1M to $3M+
$1M is undersized for a web3 platform that holds, transfers, or routes value on behalf of users. A single integration failure, missed settlement window, or smart-contract dispute can burn $1M in defense and indemnity before any settlement. Vouch's benchmark for a comparable web3 company is $3M to $5M, with a broad professional services definition and an AI-services carve-in if relevant.
2
Increase Cyber Crime / Funds Transfer Fraud to $1M+ (full limit)
The current Cyber Crime / Fraud Loss sublimit is $350K. Vouch flags this exact line as the most-negotiated item at renewal and the one carriers default low on. Treasury BEC, vendor-impersonation ACH fraud, and crypto address-poisoning attacks routinely produce $500K to $2M losses for platforms in CoinX's tier. This is a single endorsement and usually doesn't require remarketing.
3
Negotiate a cyberterrorism carve-back to the War / Cyber Operation exclusion
Endorsements WEB3 10-0069 and WEB3 10-0070 broadly exclude losses 'arising directly or indirectly from a War or arising from a Cyber Operation,' including state-directed actions. There's no cyberterrorism carve-back. Modern Cyber forms preserve coverage for cyberattacks targeted at the insured even when attribution leads back to a foreign government. Without it, *attribution disputes can be used to deny ransomware claims*.
4
Add Media Liability, Commercial Crime, and D&O
All three are Not Applicable on the declarations. Media Liability defends content, brand, and IP claims tied to public web3 promotion (blog, X, podcasts, conference talks). Commercial Crime covers employee dishonesty and forgery, which separation-of-authority controls in a small treasury function rarely close on their own. D&O is the standard backstop for venture-backed companies facing investor, regulator, and employment claims. An uninsured securities-related action is one of the more common dilution events at this stage.
5
Extend Period of Restoration from 30 days to 180 days
Every BI / CBI line is capped at a 30-day Period of Restoration. Vouch recommends a 180-day standard. Complex breach recoveries (especially when third-party cloud or blockchain-node providers are involved) routinely run past 30 days, and a short restoration period strands the tail of the income loss exactly where recovery costs spike.

03 — Your Biggest Risks

Your Biggest Risks

CoinX's risk profile is shaped by three things at once. It's a web3 platform, so it inherits crypto custody, smart-contract, and on-chain settlement exposure. It's a U.S.-domiciled platform, so it inherits SEC, FinCEN, OFAC, and state-level regulatory exposure. And, based on the policy form, it's a venture-backed early-stage company, so its loss-bearing capacity is limited and a single uninsured event can be a material equity event. The exclusion stack on the current form intersects directly with each of those three risk surfaces.

The most active enforcement bodies for web3 in 2026 are FinCEN (MSB / Bank Secrecy Act), the SEC (token classification and disclosure), the CFTC (digital commodity treatment), state Money Transmission Licensing offices, and OFAC (sanctioned-wallet screening). The form's AML, ICO, and Digital Asset Regulatory exclusions remove coverage for the most common allegations from each of those bodies. Plaintiff bars have separately driven a sustained wave of tracking-technology and biometric-statute litigation that's now overtaken ransomware as the most active source of cyber-related claims. Both are excluded on the current form.

04 — Your Limits vs. Benchmark

Your Limits vs. Benchmark

For a venture-backed web3 platform at CoinX's stage, Vouch's recommendations benchmark total combined limits at $5M to $10M. That's structured across separate Tech E&O and Cyber towers, with affirmative coverage on the lines the current form excludes. CoinX's program totals $1M per primary tower with three benchmarked lines unpurchased entirely.

Current limit Recommended range
Most consequential limit gap: Cyber Crime / Funds Transfer Fraud sublimit
The $350K Cyber Crime sublimit is the single most negotiable item on the program. Vouch flags $250K as the typical default and the $350K endorsement as incremental at best. Typical loss range for a successful BEC or vendor-impersonation attack on a web3 treasury is $500K to $2M. Pushing the sublimit to the full $1M Cyber aggregate (or higher with a dedicated Crime tower) is normally a single-endorsement negotiation and doesn't require remarketing the policy.
05 — Recommended Program Structure

Recommended Program Structure

The current program is six $1M towers with $15K to $25K retentions, riding a single carrier (United Specialty Insurance Company) through a wholesaler (Corix). The recommended structure splits Tech E&O and Cyber into separately stacked towers, fills the three unpurchased MUST HAVE lines (Media, Commercial Crime, D&O), adds a specialty digital-asset / specie layer to address the Transferor Errors and ICO exclusions, and renegotiates the most consequential exclusions before binding.
