Our Recommendation

Coinflow's Web3 Protection Policy was written when the company was smaller and before the PSP side of the business had matured. Since it was bound in May 2025, projected revenue has grown nearly 360% year-over-year — and with that growth has come meaningfully greater exposure to merchant E&O claims, regulatory scrutiny from FinCEN, SEC, and state money transmission regulators, and higher-stakes crypto custody risk. The program needs to be rebuilt around the business Coinflow actually runs today.

Three structural gaps define the renewal. The policy was designed for a general web3 startup — not a payment service provider that moves real fiat and crypto on behalf of merchants every day. As a result, the coverage that matters most to Coinflow's actual risk profile is either absent, sublimited well below the likely loss range, or explicitly excluded by form language targeting the company's core operations.

1 ADD TECH E&O — STANDALONE, $3–5M
Coinflow's API failures, missed settlement windows, integration errors, or smart contract misfires would almost certainly be framed as professional services claims — and none of those are covered under the current cyber form. This is the single largest gap in the program.
2 INCREASE CYBER CRIME / FTF / SOCIAL ENGINEERING TO $1–2M (FULL LIMIT)
The current Cyber Crime sublimit is $350K. A single BEC attack targeting a treasury employee — or a vendor-impersonation ACH diversion — commonly produces losses of $500K to $2M for a PSP at Coinflow's transaction volume. This is a single-endorsement fix and may not require remarketing the policy.
3 BUY A SPECIALTY CRYPTO / SPECIE POLICY — $2–5M
The Digital Asset exclusion in the current policy removes coverage for loss of, access to, or destruction of any digital asset Coinflow holds or transmits — including those held for clients. If Coinflow holds, escrows, or transmits digital assets, this exclusion creates a coverage cliff that a standalone specie policy from Evertas, Relm, or Coincover directly addresses.

Coinflow Labs's Biggest Risks

Coinflow's risk surface is fundamentally different from a typical SaaS or general web3 startup. Because money moves through the platform — both fiat via ACH and cards, and crypto via on-chain settlement — the company inherits both fiduciary-style exposures (custody, settlement integrity, AML compliance) and technology exposures (API failure, smart contract risk, cloud dependency). No other coverage combination in Coinflow's vertical sits at the intersection of PSP, FinTech, and web3 simultaneously.

The specific regulatory bodies — FinCEN as MSB overseer, state MTL regulators in every state Coinflow touches, the SEC and CFTC on the digital asset side, and OFAC on sanctions screening — create an unusually dense enforcement landscape for a 13-person company. The attack vectors are equally concentrated: Coinflow is a high-value, low-headcount target that processes large merchant settlements with limited internal controls separation.

Inherent risk vs. current coverage strength. Current coverage is weakest where Coinflow is most exposed.
